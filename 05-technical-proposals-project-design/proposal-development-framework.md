# Geospatial & Environmental Proposal Development Framework

A repeatable framework for preparing technically sound, feasible and evidence-led proposals in climate, land, environment and disaster risk.

## 1. Understand the opportunity
- Extract the client's problem, objectives, geographic scope and intended beneficiaries from the Terms of Reference.
- Identify mandatory outputs, duration, eligibility requirements, submission instructions and evaluation criteria.
- Build a compliance matrix linking each requirement to a proposal section and responsible contributor.
- Separate explicit requirements from assumptions that need clarification.

## 2. Frame the problem and intervention logic
- Describe the environmental or development challenge and why it matters.
- Identify the decisions the project should inform.
- Define target geography, stakeholders and intended users.
- Develop a theory of change: inputs and activities → outputs → outcomes → expected contribution to longer-term impact.
- Avoid promising outcomes outside the project's control.

## 3. Develop the technical methodology
Select methods based on the problem, data availability, scale, timing and intended decisions. Potential work packages include:
1. Inception, requirements confirmation and stakeholder mapping.
2. Data inventory, acquisition, access agreements and metadata review.
3. Data quality assessment, harmonisation and geospatial database design.
4. Remote sensing and GIS analysis.
5. Hazard, exposure, vulnerability and risk assessment where relevant.
6. Land degradation, LULC or ecosystem assessment where relevant.
7. GeoAI or statistical modelling where justified by data and validation capacity.
8. Ground-truthing, stakeholder validation and technical review.
9. Map, dashboard, report and decision-support product development.
10. Training, handover and sustainability arrangements.

Do not include every work package automatically. Tailor the method to the Terms of Reference.

## 4. Design data and analytical governance
For each dataset, record provider, custodian, access status, date, spatial/temporal resolution, coordinate reference system, licence, quality limitations and intended use. Establish metadata, versioned processing scripts, documented parameters, data lineage, access controls and backup/handover procedures.

## 5. Design quality assurance and validation
Specify quality controls before analysis begins. Examples include spatial reference and geometry checks; completeness and temporal checks; independent reference/checkpoint data; classification validation and confusion matrices; RMSE and independent checkpoints for photogrammetric products; model evaluation using appropriate held-out data; and technical/stakeholder review.

State limitations and uncertainty. Never invent accuracy thresholds or validation results.

## 6. Structure deliverables and acceptance
For each deliverable define identifier, title, content, format, due date, responsible role, review/approval process and acceptance evidence. Ensure every deliverable responds to a requirement in the Terms of Reference.

## 7. Build the implementation schedule
Sequence dependent tasks logically. Identify parallel workstreams where feasible. Include inception, data access, analysis, validation, review, revision and handover. Add milestones, dependencies, decision gates and approval periods.

## 8. Plan stakeholder engagement and governance
Identify technical owners, decision-makers, data custodians, sector institutions, local stakeholders and intended users. Define engagement purpose, timing, participation, responsibilities, feedback capture and approval authority.

## 9. Prepare the budget
Build the budget from the workplan and staffing assumptions. Typical headings include professional fees and level of effort; field travel, accommodation and subsistence; data, imagery, software and compute; field equipment and logistics; workshops and validation; training and knowledge transfer; project management; and applicable taxes.

Document units, quantities, rates, duration, currency, assumptions, exclusions and tax treatment. Map every major cost to an activity or deliverable. Verify tax rules and client requirements rather than assuming a rate.

## 10. Consolidate and quality-check
Check compliance with the ToR; alignment of objectives, methods, activities and deliverables; consistency between workplan, staffing and budget; realistic assumptions and data dependencies; clear roles; evidence and citations; confidentiality and risk management; and submission requirements.

## 11. Post-submission and mobilisation
Maintain a record of submission version, approvals, clarification questions and follow-up actions. If awarded, translate the proposal into an inception plan, work breakdown, responsibility matrix, risk register, data register and delivery calendar.

## Reusable proposal design matrix

| Component | Key question | Evidence/output |
|---|---|---|
| Problem and context | What challenge needs to be addressed? | Problem statement and baseline |
| Objectives | What should the assignment achieve? | Objectives and results |
| Data | What information is needed and accessible? | Data inventory and metadata |
| Methodology | How will the work be undertaken? | Work packages and technical workflow |
| Validation | How will quality be demonstrated? | QA plan and validation evidence |
| Deliverables | What will the client receive? | Deliverables and acceptance matrix |
| Schedule | When will each task occur? | Workplan and milestones |
| Governance | Who contributes and approves? | Stakeholder and responsibility matrix |
| Budget | What resources are required? | Costed budget and assumptions |
| Risks | What could affect delivery? | Risk register and mitigation measures |
